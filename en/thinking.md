# Architecture and Systems Thinking

<FlagshipEntry compact />

Follow the four-stage architecture learning path: [15-minute introduction](/en/articles/agent-in-15-minutes) → [ten-module intermediate guide](/en/articles/agent-ten-modules) → [runnable deep dive](/en/articles/open-etf-agent-v010-system-design) → [six-layer architecture](/en/articles/017-prompt-to-answer-six-layer-panorama-v3). Then explore ownership, authorization, evidence, failure attribution and architectural evolution. Some related essays remain available only in Chinese.

Explore the [nine core principles and six on-demand methods](/en/principles), including Context as a workbench, minimum sufficient mechanisms and replaceable capabilities. Existing essays are listed below; new and revised topics are clearly distinguished in the [writing roadmap](/en/roadmap).

**Principle essay:** [Local Improvements Are Not System Progress](/en/articles/local-improvement-is-not-system-progress) connects tool projection, tracing and evaluation to verifiable end-to-end outcomes without requiring expensive re-testing for every change.

**Reliability case study:** [How Does an Agent Continue After a Model Failure?](/en/articles/agent-provider-failover-without-resetting-the-run) covers failure domains, original Run authorization and budgets, disclosure, and recovery identity. It describes controlled continuation, not completed seamless production high availability.

**Research design lesson:** [A Research Agent Needs More Than Tools](/en/articles/etf-agent-model-harness-research-loop) shows why model judgment, settled tool receipts, time-qualified financial evidence, optional subagents, and actual delivery belong to one evidence-driven research story. It is a systems-design essay, not a finance-method qualification.

**Five editorial reading groups:** **Deep and Continuous Research**, **Subagents and Experts**, **Financial Research and Business Accountability**, **Architecture Learning Path**, and **System Boundaries and Evolution**. The learning path preserves the four-stage progression; the other groups help readers explore specific design decisions. These are navigation categories, not additional product systems or production-readiness claims. Module mechanisms and engineering validation retain their own collections.
