# Architecture and Systems Thinking

<FlagshipEntry compact />

Read how decisions about ownership, authorization, evidence, failure attribution and deletion shape a long-lived agent. Some linked essays remain available only in Chinese.

Explore the [nine core principles and six on-demand methods](/en/principles), including Context as a workbench, minimum sufficient mechanisms and replaceable capabilities. Existing essays are listed below; new and revised topics are clearly distinguished in the [writing roadmap](/en/roadmap).

**New principle essay:** [Local Improvements Are Not System Progress](/en/articles/local-improvement-is-not-system-progress) connects tool projection, tracing and evaluation to verifiable end-to-end outcomes without requiring expensive re-testing for every change.

**Reliability case study:** [How Does an Agent Continue After a Model Failure?](/en/articles/agent-provider-failover-without-resetting-the-run) covers failure domains, original Run authorization and budgets, disclosure, and recovery identity. It describes controlled continuation, not completed seamless production high availability.

**Research design lesson:** [A Research Agent Needs More Than Tools](/en/articles/etf-agent-model-harness-research-loop) shows why model judgment, settled tool receipts, time-qualified financial evidence, optional subagents, and actual delivery belong to one evidence-driven research story. Also featured in [Fintech × Agent](/en/fintech).

These are editorial subgroups within the existing architecture collection, not four new product systems or claims of production readiness. Module mechanisms and AI-assisted development remain in their separate collections.
